What it does

Operations, handled.

Appointments.

Customers text. AI checks your calendar, asks what they need, proposes times that don't conflict with what's already booked, and confirms. Walk-ins, regulars, new customers — all handled.

Customer communication.

Inbound texts, emails, and voicemails answered by AI in your voice. Common questions — prices, hours, what services you offer — handled automatically. Tougher ones escalated to you.

Customer records.

Every customer, every appointment, every transaction tracked automatically. Search by name, date, service, or anything else — AI finds it.

Inventory and supplies.

Track what's in stock, what's running low, what's out. AI drafts messages to vendors when it's time to reorder. Inventory tracking is optional — businesses that don't need it can skip it.

Reports and insights.

How was last month? Which services drive the most revenue? Which customers haven't been in lately? Ask in plain English. Get an answer.

Daily briefing.

What's on the calendar today. Who hasn't confirmed. What needs reordering. What changed overnight. Every morning, surfaced for you.

The honest answer

What if the AI says something wrong?

That's the question that keeps people from trying AI for their business. Here's how we handle it:

You teach it. You write your hours, services, and policies in plain English. The AI reads everything you wrote before responding to a customer. The more you teach it, the better it gets.

You review it. Every conversation is logged. Every booking shows up in your inbox before it's confirmed. If you don't trust it yet, set every action to require your approval — the AI proposes, you say yes.

Nothing happens without you. Sensitive actions — sending a text, processing a refund, blocking a customer — wait for your tap. The AI is your assistant, not your replacement.
